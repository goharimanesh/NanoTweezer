Numerical Modeling and Validation of an Electrostatic Nanotweezer using COMSOL Multiphysics

3D Interactive Simulation Dashboard — Small Size Structures Laboratory
Faeez Kavianpour · M. R. Gharib · A. Koochi · M. Goharimanesh

1 · Overview

Precise manipulation of nanoscale entities is a cornerstone of modern nanorobotics, nano-assembly, and single-cell biomedical investigations. Electrostatic nanotweezers are particularly attractive due to their ultra-low power consumption, microsecond-scale switching response, IC-compatibility, and absence of thermal dissipation. However, they are inherently prone to the pull-in instability — a saddle-node bifurcation where the nonlinear electrostatic force overcomes the linear elastic restoring force of the cantilever arms.

In this study, a fully coupled 3D finite element model of the benchmark Chang et al. (2009) carbon nanowire nanotweezer is developed in COMSOL Multiphysics, coupled self-consistently with the electrostatic domain via the Maxwell stress tensor and an ALE moving-mesh formulation. The predicted pull-in voltage of 99.4 V agrees with the experimental value of 101.0 V within 1.58%.

3 · Mesh Convergence Analysis

The consolidated convergence map below combines four interdependent mesh metrics into a single unified visualization: mesh type, total number of elements, total number of nodes, and the resulting final tip gap. As the mesh density increases, the final tip gap exhibits a monotonic and asymptotic decay from 4.84 μm (Coarse) to 3.26 μm (Extra Fine), while the average element quality simultaneously improves from 0.58 to 0.66.

5 · Experimental & Numerical Validation

The predicted pull-in voltage of 99.4 V differs by only 1.58% from the experimental threshold of 101.0 V reported by Chang et al. (2009), and matches the 1D numerical model of Koochi et al. (2022) exactly. The mean relative error against experimental data is 9.13%, and against numerical benchmarks only 0.62%.
